"The Equilibrium Effects of Wildfires: Evidence from the California Housing Market"
Most recent draft    Most recent slides
Click for abstract

Destructive wildfires lead to significant changes in housing markets. In this paper, I explore how wildfire exposure affects the housing market equilibrium in California. The net effect of exposure is unclear ex ante because wildfires negatively impact both housing supply and demand. I determine whether supply or demand effects are stronger by estimating the net exposure effect using 20 years of housing transaction and wildfire data. After a wildfire, prices for properties within the fire perimeter experience declines of up to 55% but fully recover to the pre-fire level three years after the fire. I explore the short-run decline by using property-level damage data and determine that the price decline is attributable to both declining housing quality and worsening local amenities. Then I show that improving neighborhood quality through increased construction and recovering local amenities helps prices to rebound. This paper finds that wildfires impact housing markets primarily through demand effects.

"Evaluating Public Spaces for Climate Adaptation: Evidence from Urban Cooling Centers"
Click for abstract

Extreme temperatures are becoming more frequent because of climate change. These temperatures can have adverse effects on human health. Individual ability to adapt to these extreme temperatures varies over both space and demographics. If publicly available infrastructure is accessible, it can help to mitigate climate damages for a larger portion of the population. In this work, I focus on government-designated cooling centers in Los Angeles County. Using administrative data on heat warnings and cooling center locations in conjunction with cell-phone mobility data, I estimate the effect of heat warnings on urban cooling center usage. On average, visitation to cooling centers does not change in times of high heat; however, the composition of users does change. Possible reasons for a lack of increase in usage include an absence of information, high travel costs, and restrictions to entry. I am currently working on implementing a travel cost model to explore if high travel costs are prohibiting usage and whether these centers are used and valued heterogeneously across space.
